BOQ gate

The BOQ is where the project becomes real

Budget, bill of quantities, and the walk-away rule — why we price after the still, never before.

2 Sep 2026 2 min read Woodex Studio · Lahore

Every cost conversation at Woodex ends at the same place: the bill of quantities. The BOQ is the document that turns a design into a number and a number into a build. Skip it, and you are not pricing a project — you are guessing in public.

Why we price after the still

A price given before the 3D gate is a price for a room that does not exist yet. Materials undecided, lighting unresolved, joinery unspecified — every open decision is a hidden cost. So the sequence is fixed: survey, design, still, then BOQ. The still is the argument; the BOQ is the receipt.

What a BOQ actually lists

Line items, quantities, and rates: demolition, civil works, electrical and plumbing, plaster and paint, flooring, joinery, fixtures, fittings — each tied to the approved drawing. When a line changes on site, you see which line changed and what it cost. A trail, not a WhatsApp scramble.

Gate factGate 4 is the lock. From the moment you approve the BOQ, the price does not move without your written instruction.

The walk-away rule

If the numbers do not work, you walk away with your design files. The build is optional. Your design is always yours. We would rather lose a construction contract than trap a client inside a budget they cannot carry — a studio that has delivered 200–500 projects can afford that rule, and a client who hears it can afford to trust the number they are given.

What moves the number

Honestly: site condition, scope of joinery, and finish level — in that order. The same 10-marla plan can carry three different budgets depending on whether the kitchen is modular or built-in, whether the wardrobes run wall-to-wall, and what the bathrooms are tiled in. These are decisions, not surprises. The BOQ makes them visible before they are irreversible.
