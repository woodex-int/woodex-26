3D gate

A still is a decision, not a picture

What the 3D gate actually produces — and why the cheapest render is the one you approve before construction.

8 Sep 2026 2 min read Woodex Studio · Lahore

The 3D gate is where the project stops being a conversation and becomes an object. You have walked the site, agreed the layout, chosen a direction — now the room appears on screen, and you get to disagree with it while disagreement is still free.

What the gate produces

One approved still, or a set: the key rooms, the facade, the finishes. The still carries the material board inside it — every surface you see is a specified surface, not a placeholder that will "be decided later." When the still is signed, the finish schedule is signed with it.

Time, in real numbers

Basic interior and exterior renders deliver in 2–5 days. A confirmed delivery date is set at the brief stage — before any work begins. Animations and virtual tours run on a programme agreed per brief, because a 30-second walkthrough is a production, not a print job.

Gate factTwo revision rounds are included in every brief. A third round is cheaper than a site change — by an order of magnitude no BOQ will soften.

What it costs

Indicatively — and we publish this because guessing helps nobody: a single interior render sits around PKR 20K–50K, an exterior render PKR 30K–80K, a 30-second animation PKR 100K–300K, a virtual tour PKR 50K–200K, and a full project visualization of 10+ renders around PKR 200K–500K. The confirmed, itemized quote comes from your brief. Scene complexity, model quality, lighting effort and animation length move the number.

The cheap render and the expensive one

The cheapest render is the one you approve before construction. The most expensive render is the beautiful one that got built wrong. The difference is rarely the render — it is whether the drawing, the still and the BOQ described the same room. At Woodex the same studio makes all three, which is the entire point of having gates.
