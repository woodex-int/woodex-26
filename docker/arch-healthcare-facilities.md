## SECTION 2 — WHAT HEALTHCARE FACILITY DESIGN COVERS

**H2:** What Healthcare Facility Design Covers

Healthcare facility design is the architectural process of planning a building for medical use — from the initial concept through to construction-ready drawings. Unlike designing a generic commercial space, healthcare architecture must respond to clinical workflows, infection control, regulatory compliance (health department, fire safety, PCATP), departmental adjacencies (reception near waiting, diagnostic near consultation, clean and dirty flows separated), and the specific requirements of medical equipment and infrastructure.

Woodex designs healthcare facilities from concept to final working drawings. We deliver concept floor plans and 3D visualizations showing the building's form, clinical zones, reception, waiting, circulation and facade — so you can see your facility before construction starts. Working drawings in AutoCAD and Revit — plans, sections, elevations, structural details, MEP coordination drawings (including medical gas, HVAC for clinical areas, power backup), schedules — the complete technical package your contractor builds from. Facade elevations designed for professional presence and wayfinding. And authority submissions: health department, LDA/CDA, fire safety — prepared and submitted as part of the service.

Every healthcare facility Woodex designs is planned around clinical requirements: patient flow (separation of clean and dirty flows, infection control), departmental adjacencies (consultation rooms near waiting, diagnostic near clinical, reception at entrance), ventilation (negative pressure rooms where required, fresh air for clinical spaces), structural requirements (medical equipment loads, radiation shielding where applicable), and regulatory compliance (health department standards, fire escape routes, accessibility).

**Checklist:**

- ✓ Clinical workflow planning (patient flow, clean/dirty separation)
- ✓ Departmental adjacencies (reception, waiting, consultation, diagnostic)
- ✓ Infection control and ventilation design
- ✓ Medical infrastructure (gas supply, power backup, drainage)
- ✓ Health department regulatory compliance
- ✓ Authority submissions (health dept., LDA, CDA, fire)

## SECTION 8 — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**H2:** Healthcare Facility Design — Frequently Asked Questions

---

**Q1: What is the difference between healthcare facility architecture and healthcare interior design?**

Healthcare facility architecture is the design of the building itself — structure, clinical zones, infection control, ventilation, medical infrastructure, authority approvals. Healthcare interior design is planning the space inside — patient rooms, finishes, wayfinding, branding. You need architecture for a new healthcare building or major redevelopment; interior design for a facility within an existing building.

---

**Q2: Do you design for infection control as part of the architecture?**

Yes. Infection control is fundamental to healthcare architecture. We plan clean and dirty flow separation, ventilation strategies (including negative pressure rooms where required), material specifications (hygienic, washable, durable surfaces), and spatial layouts that minimize cross-contamination — all designed into the building from concept stage.

---

**Q3: What medical infrastructure do you coordinate?**

We coordinate medical gas supply (oxygen, vacuum, compressed air), electrical systems (power backup, UPS, dedicated circuits for medical equipment), plumbing (medical drainage, sterilization water), HVAC (clinical-grade air handling), and structural requirements (equipment loads, radiation shielding) — all fully integrated into the architectural and working drawings.

---

**Q4: How long does healthcare facility architecture take?**

A typical healthcare facility takes 8–16 weeks from brief to final working drawings, depending on type and departments. Authority approval times are separate: health department (4–12 weeks), LDA/CDA (4–12 weeks), fire (2–4 weeks). We manage all submissions in parallel where possible.

---

**Q5: Can you design for a multi-specialty polyclinic?**

Yes. Polyclinics are one of our core healthcare types. We plan departmental adjacencies (consultation rooms grouped by specialty), patient flow (separation of specialties where needed), waiting capacity, pharmacy, diagnostic rooms, and shared infrastructure (sterilization, medical gas, power backup) — all within a single facility.

---

**Q6: What health department approvals are required?**

Healthcare facilities require health department approval for the building layout, clinical zones, infection control measures, ventilation, medical infrastructure and fire safety. The exact requirements vary by facility type (clinic vs hospital vs diagnostic). Woodex prepares all required drawings and documents and manages the submission process.

---

**Q7: Can Woodex design the building AND do the interior and clinical fit-out?**

Yes — and this is one of Woodex's key advantages. We handle healthcare building architecture, healthcare interior design, healthcare fit-out and medical furniture under one roof. The architect works directly with the interior designer and fit-out team — no re-briefing, no conflicting drawings. For everything under one contract: [Turnkey Design & Build](/turnkey-design-build/).

---

**Q8: What if I only need a front elevation for a healthcare facility?**

Woodex offers front elevation design as a standalone service — PKR 50,000–150,000. You receive a photorealistic 3D render and 2D elevation drawing. [View Front Elevation Design →](/front-elevation-design/)
