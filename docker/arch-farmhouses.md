### Farmhouse — The Bespoke Rural Escape

Farmhouse plots in Pakistan are typically located outside formal housing societies — in rural or semi-rural areas along Lahore's outskirts (Bedian Road, Canal Road extension, Babu Sabu, Kala Shah Kaku, Raiwind, Muridke, or further afield in Murree, Galyat, Swat, Naran). These plots are often much larger than urban residential plots (5–20+ kanals), irregularly shaped, and subject to different regulations (or none at all).

The design challenge is completely different from urban residential: the house must respond to the landscape, not the street. Orientation follows the view, the breeze, the sun path — not the grid. Materials may differ (exposed brick, stone, timber). The relationship between indoor and outdoor is the defining design decision. Water, electricity and sewerage infrastructure may be limited, requiring design solutions for power (solar, generator), water (bore, tank) and waste.

Woodex designs farmhouses that are rooted in their site — not generic suburban houses dropped onto rural land. Every farmhouse design begins with a detailed site analysis: topography, vegetation, views, access, orientation, prevailing wind, sun path, and available infrastructure. The house is designed in response to these conditions.

## SECTION 2 — WHAT A FARMHOUSE DESIGN INCLUDES

**H2:** What a Farmhouse Design Includes

A farmhouse design from Woodex goes beyond a standard residential architecture package. Because farmhouses are built on rural or semi-rural sites — often without the infrastructure and bylaw frameworks of housing societies — the design must address site conditions, infrastructure, and landscape as integral parts of the project.

You receive site analysis and survey (topography, vegetation, access, orientation, sun path, prevailing wind), concept design for the house in response to its site, photorealistic 3D renders showing the house in its landscape (not floating on white), working drawings for construction, and — where relevant — coordination for infrastructure: water supply, power (solar + generator), sewerage, and access roads.

The design is developed with the understanding that a farmhouse is not a suburban house in the countryside. The relationship between the house and the land is the defining element. Materials may be local (exposed brick, stone, timber). Orientation follows the view and the breeze. Outdoor living — verandahs, courtyards, terraces, pools — is designed as primary space, not afterthought.

## SECTION 3 — DESIGNING A FARMHOUSE — URBAN VS RURAL

**H2:** Designing a Farmhouse — Urban vs Rural

Designing a house on a rural or semi-rural plot is fundamentally different from designing on a standard urban residential plot. The constraints are different, the opportunities are different, and the design approach must change accordingly.

On an urban plot, the house is defined by the plot boundary, setbacks, neighbouring walls and the street. On a rural plot, the house is defined by the landscape — the view, the trees, the slope, the sun, the breeze. This is a liberating design condition, but it requires an architect who understands site-responsive design, not just floor-plan optimisation.

**Comparison: Urban Residential vs Farmhouse**

| | Urban Residential | Farmhouse |
|---|---|---|
| **Plot definition** | Plot boundary + setbacks | Landscape — views, trees, slope |
| **Orientation** | Follows the street grid | Follows the view, sun, breeze |
| **Bylaws** | LDA / CDA / society rules | Varies — may be none |
| **Infrastructure** | Municipal water, electricity, sewerage | Often self-provided: bore, solar, septic |
| **Materials** | Standard construction | Local materials possible (brick, stone, timber) |
| **Indoor-outdoor** | Minimal — courtyard or small garden | Central — verandahs, terraces, pools, landscape |
| **Access** | Paved road, urban grid | Potentially unpaved, long driveways |
| **Neighbours** | Close, visible | Distant or absent |

**Infrastructure considerations:**

| System | What to Design For |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Water Supply** | Bore well + overhead tank; filtration if required; rainwater harvesting possible |
| **Power** | Solar panels + battery storage + diesel generator backup; grid connection if available |
| **Sewerage** | Septic tank + soak pit or treatment plant; no municipal connection |
| **Access** | Approach road design, internal driveway, gate, security — all planned in the layout |

## SECTION 7 — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**H2:** Farmhouse Design — Frequently Asked Questions

---

**Q1: How is a farmhouse design different from a regular house design?**

A farmhouse is designed in response to its rural site — views, orientation, landscape, breeze — rather than the urban grid. It requires infrastructure design (water, power, sewerage) that urban houses get from the municipality. Materials may be local (brick, stone, timber). Indoor-outdoor living is central, not peripheral. The design process includes a detailed site analysis that urban residential projects don't need.

---

**Q2: What size farmhouse can I build?**

Farmhouse size depends on your plot, your budget and your brief. Common sizes range from 3,000–4,000 sq ft (compact weekend cottage) to 8,000–15,000+ sq ft (full country estate). We design to your brief and budget, not to a standard template.

---

**Q3: How much does a farmhouse design cost?**

Woodex charges PKR 800,000–2,000,000 for a complete farmhouse design — site analysis, concept, 3D renders, working drawings, infrastructure coordination. The range reflects project scale: a compact weekend cottage at the lower end, a large estate at the upper. Front elevation only is PKR 50,000–150,000.

---

**Q4: Can you design for plots outside Lahore?**

Yes. While our studio is in Lahore, we design farmhouses across Pakistan — Murree, Galyat, Naran, Swat, Abbottabad, and rural areas around Lahore. Site visits are arranged; remote collaboration keeps distance manageable.

---

**Q5: What about water and electricity on a rural site?**

These are designed as part of the project. Water: bore well + overhead tank (with filtration if required). Power: solar panels + battery storage + diesel generator backup. We assess what is available on site during the first visit and design the infrastructure alongside the house.

---

**Q6: Do I need planning permission for a farmhouse?**

It depends on the location. In many rural areas of Punjab, formal planning permission is not required for individual houses. However, some areas (cantments, notified zones, specific developments) do require approvals. We assess this during the site visit and advise accordingly.

---

**Q7: Can you design the landscape and gardens too?**

Yes. Landscape design is integral to farmhouse projects — formal gardens, orchards, pathways, outdoor seating areas, pools, boundary planting. The landscape and the house are designed as one composition.

---

**Q8: Can Woodex design my farmhouse AND do the interior design and construction?**

Yes. Woodex handles architecture, interior design, fit-out and furniture (via Woodex Furniture™) under one roof. For farmhouses, this is especially valuable — the interior design must be consistent with the exterior and the site, and the construction must follow the design intent. [Turnkey Design & Build](/turnkey-design-build/) covers everything under one contract.
