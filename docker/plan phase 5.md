# PART 1 — PAGE STRATEGY & SEO SPECIFICATIONS

## 1.1 Page Purpose and Strategic Role

The Renovation Services page is strategically different from both the fit-out pages in one critical way:

**Fit-out visitors have an empty space.**
**Renovation visitors have an existing space with problems.**

This distinction must run through every section of this page. The renovation visitor is frustrated, not excited. Their existing space is failing them — it is outdated, inefficient, poorly branded, damaged or no longer suitable for their business or lifestyle. They are not imagining a new space. They are trying to fix an existing one.

This page must speak directly to that emotional and practical reality — and then present Woodex Interior as the structured, accountable partner who will resolve it.

---

## 1.2 Visitor Profile — Renovation vs Fit-Out

```
FIT-OUT VISITOR                   RENOVATION VISITOR
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Has a new or empty space          Has an existing space
Excited about creating something  Frustrated with current state
Focused on design possibilities   Focused on solving a problem
Needs creative partner            Needs reliable problem-solver
Primary fear: wrong design        Primary fear: more disruption
Key question: What will it look   Key question: Can you do this
like when finished?               without shutting me down?
```

Every section on this page must address the renovation visitor's specific psychology — not recycle fit-out language.

## 1.6 Phased Execution — Key Strategic Differentiator

The single most important differentiator for Woodex on the renovation page is **phased execution** — the ability to renovate an occupied space without forcing the business to close completely.

This must appear prominently and repeatedly:

- In the definition section
- In the renovation types section
- In the process section
- In the cost factors section
- In the FAQ section
- In the CTA form

No competitor in the Lahore market communicates this capability clearly. It is a genuine conversion advantage.

## SECTION 7 COPY — THE RENOVATION PROCESS

**H2:**

> The Renovation Process — Step by Step

**Subtext:**

> Every Woodex Interior renovation follows the same 13-step managed process — transparent, documented and controlled from the first site visit to the signed handover certificate.

---

**Step 01 — Initial Enquiry and Brief Review**

> We begin by reviewing your renovation enquiry — understanding the sector, the approximate size of the space and the primary reasons for the renovation. This helps us assess whether a survey visit is appropriate and what specialist knowledge to bring to the site.

**Step 02 — Site Survey and Condition Assessment**

> Our project team visits the site, measures every area and conducts a thorough inspection of the existing space — recording its current condition, identifying visible defects and documenting the starting state through photography and notes.

**Step 03 — Defect Report and Scope Definition**

> Based on the survey, we prepare a defect report identifying every issue that must be addressed — structural, MEP, surface or layout — and a clear scope of works that confirms what will be done, what will be retained and what will be demolished.

**Step 04 — Space Replanning — Where Required**

> Where the renovation also involves a layout change, we develop a new space plan based on the brief — optimizing flow, function and efficiency before any new design work begins.

**Step 05 — 3D Visualization and Design Approval**

> The proposed renovation outcome is presented in photorealistic 3D — so you can see and approve the finished space before any physical works begin. Changes are made at the design stage, not during construction.

**Step 06 — Renovation BOQ and Cost Plan**

> A detailed, itemized bill of quantities is prepared — covering every demolition item, repair item, new material, trade and installation required. The cost plan is confirmed before the contract is signed.

**Step 07 — Contract, Programme and Phasing Plan**

> A formal renovation contract is issued — confirming scope, timeline, payment schedule and — where required — a detailed phasing plan showing which zones will be renovated in which sequence and how operational areas will be protected throughout.

**Step 08 — Protection and Site Preparation**

> Before any demolition begins, existing operational areas, furniture, equipment and finishes that are to be retained are protected. Hoarding, dust screens and access controls are established in accordance with the phasing plan.

**Step 09 — Strip-Out, Demolition and Civil Repair**

> Existing finishes, fixtures, joinery and MEP elements identified for replacement are stripped out and removed. Civil defects — cracks, damp, damaged surfaces and structural issues — are repaired before any new finishes are applied.

**Step 10 — MEP Modification and Upgrade**

> Electrical, HVAC and plumbing systems are modified, rerouted or replaced in accordance with the new layout and the findings of the defect survey. MEP first-fix is completed before ceilings and walls are closed.

**Step 11 — New Finishes — Ceilings, Floors and Walls**

> New ceiling systems, flooring and wall finishes are installed in accordance with the approved design and material schedule — transforming the repaired shell into the new environment specified in the 3D visualization.

**Step 12 — Joinery, Furniture and Final Dressing**

> New joinery — kitchens, wardrobes, cabinets, counters and shelving — is installed, followed by furniture placement and all final decorative and brand elements.

**Step 13 — Snagging, Quality Inspection and Handover**

> A full snagging inspection is completed — every defect, unfinished item and quality issue is documented and resolved before any handover discussion. The space is deep-cleaned, final photographs are taken and the renovation is formally handed over with complete documentation.

## SECTION 11 COPY — COST FACTORS

**H2:**

> What Affects the Cost of Renovation in Lahore?

**Intro paragraph:**

> Renovation costs are inherently more variable than new fit-out costs — because the condition of an existing space is often not fully known until the survey and strip-out have been completed. The following nine factors directly affect what your renovation project will cost.

---

**Factor 1 — Existing Condition and Hidden Defects**

> The condition of an existing space is the most unpredictable cost variable in any renovation project. Surfaces that look acceptable from the outside may conceal damp, structural defects, undersized electrical panels, corroded plumbing or inadequate waterproofing. The Woodex site survey identifies visible defects before the BOQ is issued — but some conditions only become apparent after strip-out begins. All scope changes discovered during works are documented as formal variation orders with agreed cost and timeline adjustments before additional work proceeds.

**Factor 2 — Total Area Being Renovated**

> The total square footage of the space being renovated directly affects material quantities, labour hours, programme duration and project management scope. Partial renovations of specific zones cost less than full space refurbishments — but the per-square-foot rate may be higher when working within an operational space with more complex protection and access requirements.

**Factor 3 — Scope — Partial or Full Renovation**

> A partial renovation targeting specific areas costs less in total than a complete refurbishment — but involves more complex planning to protect what is retained and coordinate phasing within an occupied space. A full renovation allows a cleaner, more efficient execution sequence — but requires the space to be vacated.

**Factor 4 — Sector Type**

> Different sectors carry different renovation cost profiles. Healthcare and pharmacy renovation carries compliance requirements and specialist surface specifications that affect both material and labour costs. Restaurant renovation involves kitchen MEP coordination and food-safe finishes. Residential bathroom renovation requires waterproofing systems that are not required in office or retail spaces. Each sector's specific requirements are assessed during the site survey.

**Factor 5 — Specification Level**

> Economy, mid-range and premium specification levels carry substantially different material and labour costs at renovation stage — just as they do in new fit-out. The choice of flooring replacement type, joinery quality, sanitary ware brand and lighting specification all affect the total project cost.

**Factor 6 — Structural or MEP Modifications Required**

> Where the existing electrical panel, plumbing stack, HVAC system or structural elements require modification, upgrade or replacement as part of the renovation scope, these items carry separate costs — often significant — beyond the surface and finish replacement. The Woodex defect report identifies MEP and structural issues before the BOQ is issued.

**Factor 7 — Kitchen or Bathroom Renovation Scope**

> Kitchen and bathroom renovation are among the most cost-intensive single-room renovation categories — because they involve simultaneous plumbing, electrical, tiling, joinery, sanitary ware and waterproofing trades working in a confined space, often with significant demolition and repair requirements before new finishes can be applied.

**Factor 8 — Phasing Requirements**

> Phased renovation — where the space must remain partly or fully operational during works — requires additional planning, more complex protection measures, temporary relocation provisions and a longer overall programme. These additional project management requirements are reflected in the BOQ as a separate phasing cost item.

**Factor 9 — Furniture and Joinery Replacement**

> Where the renovation includes complete or partial furniture replacement — workstations, reception counters, kitchen units, wardrobes, display shelving or bespoke joinery — these items are costed separately from the structural and finishing renovation scope.

## SECTION 14 COPY — FAQ

**H2:**

> Renovation Services — Frequently Asked Questions

---

**Q1: What is the difference between renovation and fit-out?**

> Fit-out refers to converting a new, empty or shell space into a complete working environment — starting from scratch. Renovation refers to improving, repairing, redesigning or refurbishing a space that already exists — working with and around existing structures, services and finishes. Many Woodex projects involve elements of both within the same scope — renovation of existing areas alongside new fit-out in expanded zones.

---

**Q2: Do I need to vacate my space during renovation?**

> Not necessarily. Woodex Interior offers phased renovation — where works are planned and executed in carefully managed zones while the rest of the space remains operational. This approach requires more detailed programming and site management, but it allows offices, pharmacies, clinics, restaurants and retail stores to continue trading throughout the renovation programme. Contact us to discuss whether phased execution is suitable for your project.

---

**Q3: Why does Woodex Interior insist on a site survey before issuing a quotation?**

> Renovation costs depend directly on the condition of the existing space — and that condition cannot be accurately assessed from photographs, videos or floor plans alone. Visible surfaces may conceal damp, structural defects, undersized electrical panels or inadequate plumbing. Without a physical survey, any renovation quotation is an approximation that is likely to be revised significantly once works begin. The Woodex site survey ensures that the BOQ is based on reality, not assumptions.

---

**Q4: What is a defect report and why is it important?**

> A defect report is a written document produced by Woodex Interior after the site survey — listing every structural, MEP and surface issue identified during the inspection. It confirms what needs to be repaired, what needs to be replaced and what can be retained. The defect report forms the basis of the renovation BOQ and prevents mid-project surprises from arising from conditions that were visible during the survey but not documented before work began.

---

**Q5: How long does a renovation project take?**

> Timeline depends on the scope, size and complexity of the project. A partial renovation of one or two zones in a commercial space may take 2–4 weeks. A full office or retail renovation of 2,000–4,000 sq. ft. typically takes 4–8 weeks. A full home renovation varies significantly based on the number of rooms, the extent of MEP modifications and the specification level. A phased renovation programme takes longer overall than a complete closure — because phases must be completed and handed over sequentially. We confirm a project-specific programme after the site survey.

---

**Q6: Will there be cost changes once the renovation starts?**

> The Woodex BOQ is based on the defect report produced after the physical site survey — which minimizes scope surprises during execution. However, renovation projects sometimes reveal conditions that were not visible during the survey and only become apparent after strip-out — for example, concealed damp behind wall finishes, damaged electrical conduit inside wall cavities or structural cracks beneath existing floor tiles. Any scope change discovered during works is immediately documented as a formal variation order with a confirmed cost and timeline impact — and agreed in writing before any additional work proceeds.

---

**Q7: Can Woodex Interior renovate my kitchen and bathroom at the same time?**

> Yes. Woodex Interior coordinates simultaneous kitchen and bathroom renovation within the same project programme — managing the sequencing of plumbing, electrical, tiling, joinery and sanitary ware trades across both rooms to minimize total project duration. Combined kitchen and bathroom renovation also reduces overall project management costs compared to commissioning two separate projects.

---

**Q8: Does Woodex Interior manage renovation projects outside Lahore?**

> Our main project team is based in Lahore. We provide renovation project delivery support in Islamabad, Karachi and other cities across Pakistan. Contact us with your project location and we will confirm resource availability and programme logistics.

---

**Q9: Can I renovate my office without upgrading the MEP systems?**

> It depends on the condition of the existing MEP systems and the scope of the renovation. Where the electrical panel, HVAC units and plumbing are in good condition and meet the requirements of the new layout, they can typically be retained. Where the renovation involves a layout change, MEP modifications are usually required to relocate outlets, extend ductwork or reposition plumbing. The Woodex site survey and defect report will confirm what, if any, MEP work is required before the BOQ is issued.

---

**Q10: What warranty or aftercare does Woodex Interior provide on renovation work?**

> Woodex Interior provides post-handover support for renovation projects. Details of the warranty terms, defect liability period and aftercare provisions are confirmed in the project contract before work begins. Contact us to discuss the specific warranty applicable to your renovation scope.
