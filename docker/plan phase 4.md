# PART 1 — PAGE STRATEGY & SEO SPECIFICATIONS

## 1.1 Page Purpose and Strategic Role

This page is a **sector-spanning hub** — different in structure from the Office Fit-Out page, which serves one client type.

The Commercial Fit-Out page must serve six distinct visitor types simultaneously:

| Visitor | Business Type                 | Primary Need                            |
| ------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| A       | Retail store owner            | Shop or store fit-out                   |
| B       | Showroom developer            | Display and brand environment           |
| C       | Restaurant operator           | Dining fit-out and kitchen coordination |
| D       | Café owner                    | Atmosphere, seating and counter fit-out |
| E       | Pharmacy owner                | Functional, compliant pharmacy fit-out  |
| F       | Clinic or healthcare operator | Healthcare-grade fit-out                |

Each visitor type must find relevant content on this page — and each must be directed toward a sector-specific deeper page where one exists.

> We develop a space plan tailored to your sector — optimizing customer flow, operational efficiency and brand presentation before any other design decisions are made.

**Step 03 — 3D Visualization and Design Approval**

> Photorealistic 3D renders bring your commercial space to life before physical work begins. You review, provide feedback and give written approval before the project advances to procurement.

**Step 04 — BOQ and Cost Plan**

> A detailed bill of quantities is prepared — itemizing every material, fixture, fitting, trade and labour element. You receive a full cost breakdown before signing any contract.

**Step 05 — Contract and Programme**

> A formal project agreement and construction programme are issued — confirming scope, milestone dates, payment schedule and the project completion date.

**Step 06 — Procurement and Lead-Time Management**

> All materials, joinery, display fixtures, lighting, flooring and furniture are procured in accordance with the programme. Lead times are tracked actively to prevent site delays.

**Step 07 — Site Mobilization and Protection**

> The site is prepared for execution — protection of adjacent areas, hoarding where required, contractor access management and site safety measures are all in place before works begin.

**Step 08 — Civil Works and Structural Elements**

> Demolition, civil repair, partition construction, shopfront installation and all structural fit-out elements are completed as the primary trade before services or finishes begin.

**Step 09 — MEP First-Fix**

> Electrical conduit, HVAC ductwork, plumbing rough-in and data conduit are installed within the structure before ceilings and walls are closed. Sector-specific MEP requirements — including kitchen ventilation, pharmacy lighting and clinic hand-wash provisions — are coordinated at this stage.

**Step 10 — Partitions, Ceilings and Finishes**

> Ceiling frameworks are installed and boarded, flooring is laid and wall finishes — paint, cladding, panels or wallpaper — are applied in accordance with the approved design.

**Step 11 — MEP Second-Fix, Joinery and Furniture**

> Light fittings, electrical accessories, HVAC grilles and plumbing fixtures are installed and tested. Custom joinery and display fixtures are installed, followed by furniture placement and dressing.

**Step 12 — Snagging, Final Clean and Handover**

> A complete snagging inspection identifies and resolves all defects before handover. The space is deep-cleaned, final photographs are taken and the completed project is formally handed over with full documentation.

## SECTION 10 COPY — COST FACTORS

**H2:**

> What Affects the Cost of Commercial Fit-Out in Lahore?

**Intro paragraph:**

> Commercial fit-out costs in Pakistan vary based on the sector, the size of the space, the starting condition and the specification level required. The following eight factors directly determine the cost of your project before Woodex Interior prepares your itemized BOQ.

---

**Factor 1 — Sector Type**

> Different commercial sectors carry fundamentally different fit-out costs. A standard retail store fit-out is generally less expensive per square foot than a restaurant, which requires kitchen ventilation, drainage and food-safe surfaces. A pharmacy or healthcare facility carries specialist compliance and surface requirements that affect both material selection and labour costs. Woodex Interior evaluates sector-specific requirements before preparing any quotation.

**Factor 2 — Total Covered Area**

> The total square footage of the space is the primary driver of fit-out cost. Material quantities, labour hours, programme duration and project management costs all scale with area.

**Factor 3 — Condition of Existing Space**

> A brand-new shell space requires less preparation than an occupied or previously fitted space needing demolition, strip-out, repair and protection of adjacent areas. The existing condition of the space is assessed during the site visit and reflected in the BOQ.

**Factor 4 — Specification Level**

> Economy, mid-range and premium specification levels carry substantially different material and installation costs. The choice of flooring type, ceiling system, lighting brand, joinery finish and facade material has a significant impact on the total project budget.

**Factor 5 — Shopfront and Facade Scope**

> Commercial spaces with street-facing shopfronts or public-facing facades require additional investment in aluminium framing systems, glazing, signage integration and exterior finishes. This scope is often substantial and is costed as a separate item in the BOQ.

**Factor 6 — MEP Complexity by Sector**

> Electrical, HVAC and plumbing requirements vary significantly between sectors. A café or restaurant requires kitchen ventilation, drainage and gas coordination in addition to standard electrical and HVAC scope. A pharmacy requires specialist lighting and storage systems. A clinic requires hand-wash stations, clinical lighting and compliance-driven MEP provisions. Each sector's MEP scope is assessed individually.

**Factor 7 — Custom Joinery and Display Fixtures**

> Reception counters, display gondolas, shelving systems, service counters, bar tops, cabinetry and bespoke joinery are costed separately from the structural fit-out. The level of customization — standard laminates versus premium veneer or lacquered finishes — significantly affects cost.

**Factor 8 — Furniture and Brand Elements**

> Seating, tables, display pedestals, branded signage, wall graphics and decorative accessories are all separate cost items. Premium imported furniture, bespoke upholstery and custom brand installations carry proportionally higher costs than standard commercial furniture ranges.
